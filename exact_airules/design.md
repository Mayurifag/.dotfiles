# Visual Design

## Direction

- Start with the subject, audience, medium, primary task, and real content; choose a visual direction that fits them,
  not a generic template. Preserve existing visual language and platform patterns unless redesign is requested.
- Make important content and actions clear. Match density to the task; group related information and controls by
  meaning, not arbitrary card boundaries.
- Build hierarchy through composition, alignment, spacing, typography, scale, and contrast. Use a cohesive palette and
  consistent type, icons, and component treatments; let emphasis and whitespace reflect importance.

## Avoid AI Defaults

- Avoid formulaic hero sections, repetitive cards or nested boxes, ornamental badges or eyebrow labels, and
  stock-looking illustrations. Use these only when they serve the brief.
- Avoid random gradients, glassmorphism, glows, neon shadows, and colored list-item accent bars; use effects
  deliberately, not as a substitute for hierarchy.
- Use specific, concise copy and meaningful icons. Avoid filler, buzzwords, and decoration that adds no information.
  Never present invented claims, metrics, testimonials, controls, or states as real; label placeholders.

## Verification

- For interactive designs, support accessible contrast, visible focus, keyboard navigation, reduced motion, and
  responsive layouts where applicable. Check relevant loading, empty, error, disabled, and unavailable states.
- Inspect the actual output at intended sizes and in context; fix issues with hierarchy, alignment, clipping,
  legibility, artifacts, and state accuracy. For images, check subject fidelity, cropping, unintended distortions, and
  rendered text. Verify interactions where applicable; report checks that could not be performed.
